Debanjan Sarkar, Ph.D. Postdoctoral Fellow, University of Texas Rio Grande Valley

I model where species can survive as their climate moves out from under them — from vultures and hornbills across Asia to locust and pollinator ranges in the Rio Grande Valley and the Himalaya — and translate that science into resilience education for the communities living in the path of change.

About

01 — Overview

I'm a postdoctoral fellow working at the intersection of ecological modeling, species distribution science, and community resilience education. My doctoral research examined the impact of climate change on India's biodiversity and protected areas under different SSP scenarios using geospatial technologies. I have 8+ years of experience across remote sensing and GIS, climate change, landscape ecology, and nature-based solutions — and outside the lab, I'm also a cartographer and mixed-media illustrator.

Current work at UTRGV spans locust and pollinator distribution modeling under future climate scenarios, and a multi-year resilience education initiative for students and community leaders in Hidalgo and Cameron counties.

Research Areas

02 — Current Work
SDM · BIOMOD2 · HPC

Locust Distribution Modeling

Species distribution modeling for Schistocerca piceifrons across the Rio Grande Valley, examining habitat overlap and hybridization zones under shifting climate envelopes. Manuscript in preparation.

25 SPECIES · SSP245 / SSP585

Pollinator Range Modeling

Multi-species distribution modeling for Lepidoptera, Hymenoptera, and Diptera pollinators across Uttarakhand and Himachal Pradesh, projected against future climate scenarios using topographic, land-cover, and bioclimatic predictors.

HIDALGO & CAMERON CO. · 3-YR

Place-Based Resilience Education

A community resilience education program running parallel Student Science and Community Leadership tracks, built with public health and conservation partners across the Valley.
